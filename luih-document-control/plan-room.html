What we learned
  • The storage model is sound. Drive holds the full history. BT holds the latest PDFs, both approved and pending: the Truss Set sits in BT's Waiting folder before approval. BT has no audit trail and no supersession.
  • Failures come from four things: release authority (who may release before the stamp), cross-sheet consistency, propagation to related documents and the field, and acknowledgement.
  • Status is copied by hand across Drive, BT, ClickUp and Slack. Luiza: "demanda muito mais atenção do que tempo" ("it demands much more attention than time"). The cost is interruption and risk, not hours.
  • Ingrid files every occurrence and does the BT uploads. Luiza submits and saves what the city returns. Both are single points of failure.
  • The folder template exists, but houses drift from it: Portuguese months in dates, a 2029 date, 17 of 20 occurrence folders spelled "Ocurrence", and no R-numbers on dated folders.
What I recommend
  • Don't buy Procore or Autodesk Build. Use BT discipline and a Bluebeam-style slip sheet now. Build a thin register plus acknowledgement in LUIH OS later.
  • Per-sheet status (WIP, for permit, released unstamped, for construction, superseded, as-built), with a V-number as the issued-for-construction handle: "we're on V8".
  • Publish records the decision and shows a status per target. BT has no public API, so BT publish is assisted or manual with a checklist.
  • On site: one QR per house, acknowledgement with escalation, auto-print with a change cover sheet, and a pre-milestone gate in Phase 1 as a manual BT checklist.
  • Close the gaps R00 missed: link cost changes to a BT change order, get buyer sign-off, distribute to trades, keep RFIs in BT, and name who may release before the stamp.
Challenge to the brief

The brief frames this as a document-management problem ("better than Google Drive"). The evidence says it's a change-control problem. Oberlin's A-8 change never reached BT. Mulbry's trusses were built from a set that contradicted itself. At Stetson the team was told to contract trusses from a set still waiting for city approval, and nobody is named as the authority for that call. A nicer folder browser fixes none of these. Release authority, a consistency check, publishing and acknowledgement do.

Structural facts we have to design around

  • Exceptions are normal. Construction sometimes builds before the occurrence exists, so the revision only records what was built. Sets get released before the stamp. Revisions get cancelled mid-flow. Some occurrences close with an EOR letter instead of going to the city.
  • "Approved" isn't "issued". The city approves, the fee is checked (and contested if wrong) and paid, the permit card arrives, then the stamped plans. Luiza ticks the BT schedule line only once she has the permit card.
  • Cities differ. Orlando accepts only the changed sheets; Winter Park and Tampa need the full book. Orlando and Orange County files are saved exactly as received. Tampa runs through a private provider, filed separately from city approvals.
  • Two people check an approved permit set before it is published: Luiza and the house's engineering coordinator.
  • The Design Book diverges from the plans by design. Its standard caveat says some layouts are "not reflected in the building drawings". Sold houses' spec books aren't locked to the version the buyer signed.
  • Acknowledgement today is a ✅ reaction. Design push posts get zero replies; field questions get 5 to 12 and get resolved.
  • Pieces that already exist: Ticket Hub, ClickUp D&P Phases 7.3–7.9 (they track the folder states closely, but occurrence tickets use a second status field), Specbook versioning in LUIH OS with a Slack bot, a BT→ClickUp read connector, the internal review checklist, the Submission Checklist and the Comment Agent. A QR already prints on the plans and points to Luiza's narrative page.
  • No "D0" label exists in Drive. The written standard is R0# - mmm.dd.yyyy, but none of the dated folders checked carry an R-number.

Naming standard
  • Sheet file: <SHEET>_<HOUSE>_R<nn>_<YYYY-MM-DD>.pdf. One R-number per sheet revision, never reused; the register enforces it.
  • Package: PKG-<nn>_<YYYY-MM-DD>_<TRIGGER>_<scope>, where TRIGGER is PERMIT, CITY-Cn, OCC-<ClickUp id>, RFI-<n> or AS-BUILT.
  • Set version: V<n> per house. It bumps only when a for-construction or released-unstamped sheet changes. V8-P means it contains unstamped sheets.
  • ISO dates only. This removes Out.05.2026, Dez.08.2025, Sep.08.2029 and Approved_dec_13 in one rule.

Choice I made, open to challenge: 01. Approved is the city-stamped record, as received, because that is what the inspector checks, and the stamped set and the construction set legitimately differ (two electrical sets, Sep 14). "Current for construction" lives in 02. Construction Documents and the register. Today both patterns exist: 907 N Hyer keeps dated approved snapshots, 1225 Golfview keeps flat current sheets. Apply the standard to new houses and pre-framing active houses only, starting in Phase 1. Nothing moves during discovery.

P-1

LUIH OS feature · "Plan Room"

Plan Room is a thin register plus acknowledgement per house inside LUIH OS. It follows the principle in PRD F03/F04 and the TRFC: the platform is the source of truth, ClickUp and Slack announce, and Drive is the distribution copy. Files stay in Drive and BT. LUIH OS records which sheet revision is current, its status, why it changed, and who has acknowledged it. It checks BT against the register every week instead of writing to BT.

Why this beats Drive for Ops Better
  • A sheet register, not folders. One row per sheet with its current revision and status. "What's the latest?" becomes a glance.
  • Per-sheet status beside the V-number (ISO 19650-lite): WIP · for permit · released unstamped · for construction · superseded · as-built. The city-stamped record and the construction set can differ without hiding it.
  • Linked-sheet prompt. Change C-1 and Plan Room suggests L-1, starting from the rules Ingrid already applies (drainage → landscape; pool deck → site plan → sheet 2) and learning from past packages.
  • Package timeline that reads and writes ClickUp D&P phases 7.3–7.9 and the ticket's Design & Permit Status.
  • Visible exceptions. Released before stamp (with authorizer and expiry), as-built and cancelled are real states with owners.
  • Audit trail for plans: who published what, when and why. A weekly BT listing check flags BT edits the register didn't make.
Build vs buy
  • Procore Drawings / Autodesk Build: no. Roughly $10K–$80K a year by third-party estimate. They add a second field app beside BT for a radio-and-paper superintendent, still need a BT sync, and have no acknowledgement. Revisit only if a quote with native BT sync comes in under ~$10K a year, or BT is replaced.
  • Bluebeam-style slip sheet on the design desk: yes, now. It marks superseded pages and carries change clouds for the 2–3 people who produce sets, with no code.
  • BT discipline: yes, now. Upload permissions per audience, notification to subs on upload, client change orders and RFIs are already paid for.
  • LUIH OS: build only the register and acknowledgement. Nobody sells acknowledgement or the Specbook join. Most plumbing already exists in LUIH OS: the Specbook distribution orchestrator, version schema, Slack changelog, house-channel lookup, Drive replace-in-place and the ClickUp adapter.

P-3

Getting the latest version to site

The constraint is cultural: Mike's world runs on radio, paper and phone, and the job channel only works when the field pulls. Paper stays, but paper has to tell you when it's out of date, and the people who build from the sheets, including trades, have to confirm before they fabricate. Options are ranked by leverage relative to effort.

#IdeaHow it worksPillarEffort
1Pre-milestone gate (Phase 1)A manual checklist item in the BT schedule before truss order, framing start and MEP rough: "current V acknowledged by super, PM and the affected trade". It prevents an incident instead of recording one. This would have caught Mulbry's 9' trusses.CheaperLow
2Acknowledge plus escalationSlack button or a BT daily-log line: "Plans updated to V8". Sent only once BT is confirmed. No acknowledgement in 24h escalates to the construction manager.BetterLow
3Automatic print with a change cover sheetThe changed sheets and a one-page "what changed" (clouded deltas, reason, trades affected) print in the office. Swap them in the blue bag and return the old ones.FasterLow
4One QR per houseOn the blue bag cover, the job sign and the change cover sheet. It opens the current set. No sealed PDF is touched. First check what the existing QR on the plans links to.BetterLow
5Superseded stampBluebeam-style slip sheet on the design desk marks the old page "SUPERSEDED — see V8". Apply it to the superseded copy only, and confirm with the EOR.BetterLow
6Site tablet showing the current setA ruggedized tablet in the blue bag pinned to the BT current-set view. Only worth it once options 1–4 are adopted.FasterMedium
—QR on every sheetOut of v1. Stamping a QR into a digitally sealed PDF can invalidate the Florida seal, and the behaviour evidence says nobody will scan out of curiosity. Revisit if per-house scans exceed one per house per week.Out of v1—

Recommendation: ship 1 to 4 together in Phase 1. The gate is the only control that stops wrong work. Acknowledgement and paper close the loop for the super and PM, and the per-house QR gives anyone a way to check. Construction leadership has to agree to enforce the gate.
